A federal notice just tripled one grant's award floor and retired the Alaska-only program it belonged to.

On July 28th the Administration for Native Americans retired SEDS Alaska. Two million dollars, three awards, and a floor of one hundred thousand. That's a sum one village administrator can actually run.

The money didn't shrink. EAGLE, the successor, carries twenty four million dollars and thirty one awards. Alaska Native communities stay eligible. The agency said so in writing.

EAGLE's floor is three hundred thousand. EAGLE is national. The smallest door tripled and the field went from Alaska to the whole country.

That same notice built the AI3 Action Institute. One award. Nationwide. A floor of two and a half million dollars. Applications close August 27th.

The case for doing that is real. Alaska has two hundred twenty nine federally recognized tribes. The agency is folding four training and technical assistance contracts into a single cooperative agreement, which it says will streamline operations. One body doing that once is better than everyone doing it separately.

The agency asked for comment and got thirty two submissions. It printed their objections in its own notice. Opportunity costs. Indigenous data sovereignty. Centralized program design.

The notice says the institute will complement, not replace, broader social and economic development efforts.

Alaska already did the opposite. A three hundred eighty thousand dollar grant at the University of Alaska Anchorage asked any Alaskan organization to name its own problem. Twelve wrote one down. Nome Joint Utilities System asked about thermal conductivity in warming permafrost. The Village of Alakanuk Tribe asked about infrastructure coordination. Four were funded. Eight went unfunded.

The institute isn't the mistake. Retiring the small Alaska door in the same notice is.

If an Alaska village leads the winning bid when applications close on August 27th, I'm wrong, and I'd like to be.

If somebody asked you to name the problem yourself, what would you put on the line?
