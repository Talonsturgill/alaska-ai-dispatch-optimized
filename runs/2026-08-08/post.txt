The feds designed this money in a way that is almost directing us to AI, a state legislator said. Then it bought X-ray machines.

Alaska got 272 million dollars this year from a federal program called Rural Health Transformation. Rep. Genevieve Mina, who sits on its advisory council, said that back in June.

The first awards landed on August 7th. Nineteen projects. Over 4.5 million dollars, which is under two percent of the year's money.

Chugachmiut, an Alaska Native nonprofit, got 627,200 dollars for portable X-ray machines in five rural clinics. Norton Sound Health Corporation got 292,100 dollars for a new scheduling system. Turnagain Community Health in Girdwood put 100,000 dollars toward a pharmacy testing medication kiosks.

Not one of the awards the Anchorage Daily News described uses artificial intelligence.

Three things cut against reading much into that. It is week one, and more than 267 million dollars is still undecided. Kodiak, the Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta and the North Slope got nothing in this round. The state's own award spreadsheet would not open for us, so every award figure here comes from one newspaper's reporting.

Then I went and read the federal law. Artificial intelligence appears in it exactly once. Not under equipment. Not under purchases. It sits inside a clause about training and technical assistance. Training people.

Mina read the pressure correctly. The rules really do bar new construction and broadband, and they cap capital at twenty percent of the award, which pushes money away from anything you have to build. The law just puts AI somewhere almost nobody looks for it.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. A state chasing AI procurement and a state building people who can run the tools will spend the same money and land somewhere very different by 2027.

The next batch goes out Friday.

If it were yours, would you buy the tools or train the people?
