A federal notice just tripled the smallest grant an Alaska applicant can win, and almost nobody noticed.

On July 28th the Administration for Native Americans retired SEDS Alaska. Two million dollars, three awards, and a floor of one hundred thousand. That is a sum one village administrator can actually run.

The money didn't shrink. EAGLE, the successor, carries twenty four million dollars and thirty one awards. Alaska Native communities stay eligible. The agency said so in writing.

EAGLE's floor is three hundred thousand. EAGLE is national. The smallest door tripled and the field went from Alaska to the whole country.

That same notice built the AI3 Action Institute. One award. Nationwide. A floor of two and a half million dollars. Every AI dollar it created sits in a single grant no village can lead. Applications close August 27th.

Here is the case for doing exactly that, and it's a good one. Alaska has two hundred twenty nine federally recognized tribes. Picture all of them separately arguing with one vendor over their own data. One body doing that once is better, and a hundred thousand dollar grant will never buy a sovereignty framework.

The agency asked for comment and got thirty two submissions. It printed their objections in its own notice. Opportunity costs. Indigenous data sovereignty. Centralized program design.

So what is that institute paid to produce? A list of where AI could help Native communities.

Alaska already made one. A three hundred eighty thousand dollar grant at the University of Alaska Anchorage asked any Alaskan organization to name its own problem. No menu. Twelve wrote one down. Nome Joint Utilities System asked about thermal conductivity in warming permafrost. The Village of Alakanuk Tribe asked about infrastructure coordination. Four were funded. Eight still hold a list nobody has published.

The institute isn't the mistake. Retiring the small Alaska door in the same notice is.

If an Alaska village leads the winning bid on August 27th, I'm wrong, and I'd like to be.

If somebody asked you to name the problem yourself, what would you put on the line?
