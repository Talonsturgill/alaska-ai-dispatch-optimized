Alaska's Division of Elections ran its routine quarterly automated comparison of the
voter file against Division of Motor Vehicles records. It flagged roughly 3,500
registered Alaskans as possible noncitizens, far above the usual return, and swept up
people who are in fact naturalized US citizens. Notices went out four weeks before the

Status: two independent adversarial validators are working. NOTHING below is cleared

## THE HONEST AI ANGLE
Say plainly on screen that this is NOT artificial intelligence. It is a database
comparison between two state systems that were never built to talk to each other. That
is the point, not a disclaimer: it is precisely the failure mode people fear from
automated decisioning, already running at scale on Alaskans today. A confident
automated output, a stale reference dataset, no human adjudication before the letters
went out, and the burden of proof flipped onto the citizen.

Note the shape INVERTS yesterday's dispatch rather than repeating it: 2026-07-25 was a
detector that failed to fire (false negative). This is a detector that fired wrongly
(false positive).

## FAIRNESS DISCIPLINE (non-negotiable on this one)
- The Division of Elections is NOT the villain. List maintenance is a legal obligation.
  Represent their strongest defense in their own words.
- Do not imply anything about how common noncitizen voting is beyond what the
  validators verify. This is the most politically loaded number in the piece.
- Keep "flagged" separate from "moved to inactive" separate from "removed" separate
  from "cannot vote". Never blur them for drama.
- No partisan framing. The people harmed here are naturalized citizens of every
  politics, and legislators of more than one party raised concerns.
