## VERDICT: the AI framing does not survive contact with the sources.

The words "AI", "artificial intelligence", and "machine learning" appear in NONE of the
three primary sources (NOAA survey page, ahab.aoos.org/ifcb, aoos.org bloom article).
The only automation language anywhere is one sentence: "an algorithm has been developed
to automatically flag harmful algal cells". The classifier is unnamed. The same
paragraph says humans still review manually and the model "will continue to be refined
in the future", which is prototype language.

Worse, the page carrying every technical spec has NO publication or last-updated date.
Its footer reads 2022 and its photos are captioned July 2024. It cannot carry a 2026

Validator's strongest counterpoint, quoted because it is the thing to beat:
"Framing this as an Alaska AI win overstates the technology, the novelty, the

Alaska's contribution is funding two units and lending a boat.

- No PSP fatality rate or Alaska death count survived (CDC 404, NOAA 404, DEC 403).

## LESSON FOR THE ROUTINE
A story can pass dedupe, have great story fuel, and still be built on an undated page.
Check the DATE and the ACTUAL WORDING of the load-bearing source before locking a story,
not after. The Phase 2 gate did its job here, but one phase earlier would have been
cheaper.

## THE CRUX, verified by THREE independent sources, two peer-reviewed
"Only commercially harvested shellfish intended for interstate commerce are regularly
tested for STXs. Since the State of Alaska does not test recreationally or
subsistence-harvested shellfish, some Native communities have established their own
testing procedures."
  -- Litaker, Matweyou, Kibler, Hardison, Holland, Tester, Toxins (Basel), 2025-05-28

"Despite the importance of shellfish harvesting to Native cultures, subsistence and
recreational shellfish toxins are not routinely monitored or addressed by the State of
Alaska." -- Roland et al., Ethnicity and Disease, 2022-10-20

"The Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation testing program ensures that
shellfish reaching the retail food market are safe. But no testing program exists for
recreational or subsistence harvests." -- Alaska Sea Grant, UAF

NOTE: dec.alaska.gov returns 403 host-wide to this environment. Do NOT put quoted words
in DEC's mouth. The claim survives on the three sources above without DEC.

## WHO FILLS THE GAP (tribal-led, and that is the story)
SEATOR, the Southeast Alaska Tribal Ocean Research network, formed 2013, LED BY THE
SITKA TRIBE OF ALASKA (a federally recognized tribal government) through its Sitka
Tribe of Alaska Environmental Research Lab. Per NOAA NCCOS: partners include 16
communities between Kodiak, Yakutat and Metlakatla, weekly sampling at over 52
monitoring sites, over 800 shellfish samples analyzed annually. (Vintage: NCCOS project
period 2019-2022. Label it or soften it. seator.org and sitkatribe.org are JS-rendered
and returned empty, so current counts are UNVERIFIED.)

Distinct from AOOS (a regional ocean observing organization) and the Alaska HAB Network
(multi-partner, formed 2017). Keep all three separate on screen.

## THE HONEST TURN, and it is excellent
Detecting the bloom is NOT the same as knowing the beach is safe. Butter clams, a
staple subsistence species in Southeast, may retain saxitoxins for TWO YEARS OR MORE
after the bloom is gone. Prior work found no correlation between clam toxin content and
beach terrain, tidal magnitude, water temperature, or daylight.
  "This reinforces the reality that without testing, it is impossible to predict when a
  highly toxic clam will be harvested." -- Litaker et al., Toxins, 2025
And from the tribal-lab side, co-authored by Sitka Tribe and SEATOR staff:
  "butter clam toxin concentrations in Southeast Alaska often display significant
  temporal variability that is inadequately explained by seasonal HAB dynamics."
  -- Harley et al., Toxins, 2024-10-29
PICTURE: a bloom flagged offshore in green, a clean line drawn to a beach, then the line
BREAKS, and a clam on that beach still glows red two years after the bloom is gone.
Coverage arithmetic on screen: TWO IFCBs for the whole state against 33,904 miles of
shoreline.

## SCALE, verified primary
- Chukchi Alexandrium cyst beds total 145,600 km2, 6.3x larger than the Gulf of Maine
  bed previously considered the world's largest. Mean bottom temperatures in the Ledyard
  Bay cyst bed area rose from 4.2 to 6.8 degrees C (1999-2003 vs 2014-2018), which the
  authors calculate enhanced germling cell production 1.6- to 1.8-fold and cut time to
  germination by about 20 days. -- Anderson et al., PNAS, 2021-10-04
- Alaska has 6,640 miles of coastline and 33,904 miles of shoreline including islands.
- PSP: saxitoxins; symptoms usually appear 30-60 minutes after eating; NOT destroyed by
  cooking, canning, freezing, pickling, salting or smoking (CDC Yellow Book 2025). "Eating
  a single shellfish, such as a clam, that is contaminated with PSP toxin has the
  potential to cause death." (Alaska Sea Grant, UAF)
- Annual reported Alaska PSP cases ranged from 4 to 17, 2011-2021 (Litaker et al. 2025).
  USE THIS, not the 132-cases/5-deaths figure, which is secondhand via ADN only.
